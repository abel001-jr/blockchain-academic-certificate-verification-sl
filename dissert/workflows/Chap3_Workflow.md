## 4. Step 3: Establish the analysis baseline

Describe current actors, process steps, inputs, outputs, approvals, existing controls and exceptions. Label participant-reported steps when observation is unavailable. Identify problems through records or accounts rather than treating paper use or a centralized database as proof of insecurity.

Separate active staff handling time from elapsed calendar turnaround. If no defensible baseline exists, do not promise a measured percentage reduction in delay or workload. Identify the affected stakeholders and the information needed for each verification task.

Prepare a current-process summary and a problem-to-proposed-response mapping. These support Sections 3.6 and 3.7 and provide the basis for requirements, even though their final placement comes later in the chapter.

## 5. Step 4: Build the requirements register

Create a row for each candidate requirement using the fields in the companion guide. Link it to an objective, evidence source and problem. Confirm its priority and testability. Mark requirements derived only from Chapter Two as candidates until the case analysis supports them.

Functional coverage should address authorized actors, approval, final issuance, document commitments, encrypted storage, graduate ownership/access association, public lookup, confidential sharing, expiry, revocation, failure outcomes and relevant auditing. Include only functions within the approved scope and explain justified exclusions.

Quality coverage should address authorization/privacy, integrity, usability, performance, fault handling, maintainability and state recovery where applicable. Specify the operation, environment, metric and acceptance rule. Unverified five-second or 80% targets in the earlier draft remain proposals; record a rationale before adopting them.

For every acceptance check, identify a planned test reference. Reserve actual pass/fail outcomes for Chapter Five. Avoid claiming universal security from a finite negative-test set.

## 6. Step 5: Write the methodology and gathering account

Explain why the actual development method fits the project and which outputs each stage produced or is intended to produce. If Design Science remains, explain its role separately and add a verified methodological source. Do not label development Agile or iterative merely because changes occurred.

State participant eligibility, recruitment, techniques and instruments. Keep administrative interviews, graduate requirements responses and prototype usability participants separate. Report actual denominators and exclusions when records support them. Explain how data will be or was analyzed and how resulting needs become requirements.

Document consent, access restrictions, retention, reporting and confidentiality. Job titles can identify staff in small offices; do not promise anonymity that the reporting format cannot provide. Use synthetic records for technical demonstration unless separate authorization supports another procedure.

## 7. Step 6: Draft in dependency order

Draft the evidence-backed analysis, requirements and proposed response first. Then complete the development method and requirements-gathering account. Write the introduction and summary last. Present the completed chapter in the eight-section sequence in the guide, regardless of drafting order.

| Requirements-gathering sources and instruments | Connect actors and techniques to the evidence obtained or planned. |

Number tables in first-appearance order after the chapter is assembled. Keep extensive interview forms, raw data and test logs in appendices or supporting records. Add a workflow figure only where it improves understanding; the guide does not require decorative diagrams.

## 8. Step 7: Check consistency and document quality

Check that all four Chapter One objectives have a methodology, requirement or evaluation link. Preserve Chapter Two's distinction between established techniques and the case-specific contribution. Maintain the public/confidential verification scopes and the local-prototype/consortium distinction.

Review every claim of completed work against the evidence register. Verify methodological citations from original sources and supply full reference entries. Remove editorial instructions from the chapter body, retaining unresolved tasks in revision notes. Generate only the Chapter Three Word copy when the actual revision is authorized; do not regenerate unrelated chapters.

Apply the handbook's formatting and verify text parity, headings, table references and package integrity. Complete visual review in Word before treating the output as submission-ready.

## 9. Deliverables and acceptance gate

The next revision should produce the revised chapter in Markdown and Word, an evidence/requirements register and a short revision record. The original Chap3.docx remains preserved. Detailed material earmarked for later chapters remains traceable rather than silently discarded.

Proceed to chapter editing once this structure and its evidence rules are established. Accept the revision only when all six supervisor-required areas are explicit, requirements are assessable, claims reflect their evidence status, source references agree and chapter boundaries are clear. Remaining institutional facts or empirical results must stay unresolved until their records are available.
